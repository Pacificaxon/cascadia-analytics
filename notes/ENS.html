Executive summary

We initiate coverage on EnerSys (NYSE: ENS) with a BUY rating and a $220 base-case target, implying 30% upside from the current $168.72. EnerSys is the global leader in stored energy solutions for industrial applications, operating through three segments: Energy Systems (telecom, broadband, data center backup power), Motive Power (forklift batteries and chargers), and Specialty (aerospace, defense, medical).

The market is pricing in an industrial recession, valuing ENS as if revenue will stagnate at a 2% CAGR with compressing margins. We see this as a mispricing. Management recently disclosed that the Aerospace & Defense backlog surged 27%, with the munitions backlog up 230% year-to-date. The Specialty segment is converting this backlog into revenue at an accelerating pace. Two company-specific catalysts sharpen the setup. EnerSys secured about $150M in DOE grant funding toward a $650M defense-focused lithium cell campus in Greenville, SC (construction H1 FY28), anchoring its missile-defense and munitions power backlog in domestic supply. On data centers, the DataSafe Noir lithium platform has 100 systems on order and 500+ units quoted to suppliers, with revenue inflection expected by fiscal 2028, while legacy lead-acid data center solutions still guide high-single to low-teens growth. Meanwhile, AI data centers with rising power density requirements are driving demand for advanced backup battery ecosystems, a market where ENS holds a narrow-to-wide moat through switching costs and its proprietary Thin Plate Pure Lead (TPPL) technology.

Q2 FY2027 results (September quarter) showed $935.6M in sales (+4.8% YoY), a 16.2% adjusted operating margin, and $217.8M in free cash flow. Net leverage sits at a comfortable 1.3x EBITDA. IRC Section 45X domestic manufacturing tax credits are structurally boosting gross margins and free cash flow, a benefit the market has not fully credited.

Our read: The market sees a cyclical forklift battery company. We see a defense and data center power infrastructure play trading at an industrial multiple. The 2.1% revenue CAGR implied by the current price assumes the A&D backlog and AI power demand simply do not materialize. That is too pessimistic.

ENERSYS (ENS)KEY THEMES / KPIS / GUIDANCE

Key themes

1. Defense lithium campus de-risks the A&D rampACCELERATING

EnerSys secured roughly $150M in Department of Energy grant funding for a $650M defense-focused lithium cell manufacturing campus in Greenville, South Carolina, with construction slated for the first half of fiscal 2028. The campus anchors the munitions and missile-defense backlog in domestic supply, EnerSys supplies power for missile defense, soldier power, and munitions (not the weapons systems themselves).

2. DataSafe Noir targets AI data center demandACCELERATING

The DataSafe Noir lithium battery platform is purpose-built for AI data center power density. EnerSys has 100 systems on order and over 500 units actively quoted to data center suppliers, with meaningful revenue inflection expected by fiscal 2028. Legacy lead-acid data center solutions continue to project high-single to low-teens revenue growth, these systems retain strong staying power alongside the lithium ramp.

3. A&D Backlog ConversionACCELERATING

Specialty backlog +27%, munitions +230% YTD. Revenue conversion is the key metric to watch over the next 4 quarters.

4. IRC 45X Tax CreditsSTABLE

Domestic manufacturing credits structurally boost FCF. Benefit is recurring as long as US production continues.

5. Motive Power NormalizationSTABLE

Book-to-bill at 0.9x as the segment returns to pre-COVID levels. Not growing, but stabilizing. The growth segments (defense lithium, data center) now carry the forward story while Motive Power acts as a cash-generative stabilizer rather than a growth driver.

6. Operational OptimizationSTABLE

Monterey plant closure ahead of schedule. Cost actions support margin expansion toward the 13-14% normalized range.

Risks to our BUY

  • Industrial cyclicality: Motive Power is exposed to warehouse and manufacturing capex cycles; a deep recession would pressure volumes.
  • Customer concentration: Loss of a major telecom or hyperscale customer would impact Energy Systems.
  • Lead price volatility: Lead is a key input; sharp price increases compress margins if not passed through.
  • Defense budget shifts: Changes in US defense spending priorities could slow A&D backlog conversion.
  • Competition: East Penn, Exide, and GS Yuasa compete on price in commoditized segments.
