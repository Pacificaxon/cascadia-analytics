Headline

The undisputed monopolist of advanced lithography is executing flawlessly, but the market is pricing in a growth trajectory that exceeds even our bull case. We rate ASML a Hold on valuation.

Executive Summary

We are initiating coverage on ASML Holding N.V. with a Hold, Valuation Concerns rating and a 12-month base case target of $1,415, implying 13.8% downside from the current $1,642.

ASML maintains an absolute monopoly in Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) lithography, making it the indispensable chokepoint for global advanced semiconductor manufacturing. Q3 2026 was a record across the board: net sales of 11.7 billion euros (up 55% year over year), gross margin of 57.5%, and net income of 5.0 billion euros. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance for the third time, now expecting 44 to 45 billion euros in sales with approximately 56% gross margin.

The business is exceptional. The valuation is the problem. Our reverse DCF shows the market is pricing in a 21.9% revenue CAGR over the next six years, above our bull case of 18.5%. At a live CAPM-derived WACC of approximately 9.4% (beta 1.30 decaying to 1.00, 10-year at 5.1%, implied ERP at 3.7%), the math does not support the current price even under optimistic assumptions. The market is adequately pricing in standard EUV demand and then some, leaving no margin of safety for the inevitable cyclicality of semiconductor capital equipment.

In our view, investors should wait for a better entry point. The company is a generational compounder, but price matters.

Bull Case

High-NA EUV ramp accelerates. TSMC, Intel, and Samsung are moving faster than expected on 2nm and sub-2nm nodes. Each High-NA system commands 350 to 400 million euros, dramatically above standard EUV pricing, driving both revenue and margin upside.

Memory capex supercycle. DRAM makers are structurally increasing capital intensity for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) used in AI accelerators. Management guides memory system sales up approximately 75% in 2026. This is the most capital-intensive memory build in history.

Installed Base Management compounding. The high-margin service and upgrade business hit 3.3 billion euros in Q3, beating guidance by nearly 400 million euros. This recurring stream carries accretive margins and grows with the installed base regardless of new system cycles.

Demand visibility through 2030. Customers are already discussing equipment needs for 2029 and 2030. Management has stated that planned 2027-2028 capacity may not meet demand. Backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility that few industrial companies can match.

Our bull case ($1,780, +8.4%) assumes 18.5% revenue CAGR through 2031 with terminal FCF margins reaching 40%.

Bear Case

Valuation leaves no room for error. At 21.9% implied revenue CAGR, the market expects ASML to compound faster than our most optimistic scenario for six straight years. Any disappointment in High-NA timing, memory capex, or China demand would compress the multiple sharply.

China export risk. China is expected to account for approximately 20% of 2026 sales, but tightening export controls on DUV systems create a structural headwind. Further restrictions could remove a meaningful revenue contributor.

Cyclicality is real. Semiconductor equipment has always been cyclical. The current AI-driven upcycle is powerful, but WFE spending will eventually normalize. ASML traded at far lower multiples during the last downturn.

Capital intensity. The company is spending heavily to expand High-NA manufacturing capacity. If demand disappoints, these fixed investments pressure returns.

Our bear case ($1,161, -29.3%) assumes 10.5% revenue CAGR with terminal FCF margins of 32%.

Verdict

ASML is the highest-quality company in semiconductor capital equipment, with a moat that may be the widest in all of technology. But quality and value are different things. At $1,642, we see 13.8% downside to our base case of $1,415. The reverse DCF verdict is unambiguous: the market is pricing in euphoric growth assumptions.

We rate the stock Hold, Valuation Concerns. We would become constructive on a meaningful pullback toward our base case, where the risk/reward would turn favorable for a business of this caliber. Until then, we see better opportunities elsewhere in the semiconductor value chain.
