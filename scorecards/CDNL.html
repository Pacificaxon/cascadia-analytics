Top Risk Factors (Item 1A)

1. Margin Collapse: Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 12.4% in Q2 from 18.6% a year ago and GAAP gross margin printed 10.8%, against a full-year margin guide management cut to 16-18% from 20%+ in the same release that raised revenue guidance. The company's own account assigns the pressure to subcontracted labor and equipment rental costs in developing markets, a mix shift toward larger commercial and industrial work, accelerated G&A spending, and Southeast weather. The self-performing cost advantage the equity story rests on did not show up in the quarter. [8-K]

2. Leverage on a Small Base: The $142M net cash position is the June 30, 2026 filed balance sheet: $197M of debt against $339M of cash. About $88.9M went out for Allied Paving on October 1, and the Hughes Brothers cash portion of $214.5M is funded from cash on hand and credit facility borrowings, with a $250M delayed-draw term loan added in September. The forward balance sheet carries debt the June anchor does not show. [10-K FY25]

3. Cyclical + Integration Risk: Civil and site development is cyclical, weather-exposed, and concentrated in the Southeast. Integration evidence is mixed: Q1 commentary called ALGC a strong contributor from day one, while the Q2 10-Q cut the acquired ALGC backlog intangible by $5.1M on revised backlog revenue and completion schedules. Plaintiff firms now allege scalability problems at ALGC were not disclosed; the company has not reported them. [10-K FY25]

4. Hughes Brothers Dilution and Earnout: The amended Hughes Brothers agreement pays $357.5M at 60% cash and 40% stock: 5,255,420 shares priced at the $27.21 signing VWAP while the market trades near $14.01. The earnout adds up to $162.5M, payable 60% in cash and 40% in stock, on Hughes adjusted EBITDA for the year to June 30, 2027, and up to 2,388,827 further shares at the maximum. Closing is expected in Q4 2026 subject to HSR clearance, so none of this is in the current share count or balance sheet, and all of it lands at once. [8-K]

5. Litigation Overhang: Hagens Berman (September 10), Lowey Dannenberg (September 17) and Scott+Scott (October 14) have announced investigations into potential federal securities law violations, centered on the June 24 secondary offering of 4.6M shares at $73.00 and on whether project cost pressures were disclosed while margin guidance stood above 20%. No finding has been made against the company; the overhang itself keeps institutional buyers sidelined and raises the cost of the next capital raise. [PR]

6. Backlog Deceleration: Backlog reached a record $866M at June 30, but growth slowed to +35% year over year from +60% at March 31, and the 10-Q expects $734M to $812M of it to convert within twelve months. For a company valued on hyper-growth, a halving of backlog growth makes new awards lumpier and removes the forward cover the multiple rested on. [8-K]
